## 👋 What Is This?

Have you ever wanted to turn a photo or drawing into something you can put on a website? **image-to-css-art** does exactly that. It's a smart helper tool that looks at any reference image on your computer and turns it into a beautiful piece of art made entirely from HTML and CSS code — no heavy image files needed. Your pictures become lightweight, clear, and scalable illustrations that live right inside a single webpage file.

You can use it for creating fun website decorations, unique profile pictures, custom icons, or just to see your favorite photo transformed into something totally new and retro-cool.

- **🔍 Output Audit** – Shows you a detailed report of what was created, how many elements were used, and how accurate the color matching is, so you always know what you're getting.

- **🎛️ Customizable Levels** – You can choose how detailed or simple you want your final artwork to be, from blocky minimalism to rich complexity.

## 🖥️ Tips for Best Results

- **Pick images with high contrast** – Photos with strong differences between light and dark produce bolder, more striking art.
- **Try simple shapes first** – Start with a basic icon or logo to get comfortable before trying complex photos.
- **Adjust the detail level** – If your art looks too busy, lower the detail setting. If it looks too simple, raise it.
- **Use bright colors** – Vivid images tend to make the most visually appealing CSS art.
- **Experiment with different images** – There's no wrong answer. Just have fun exploring the creative possibilities.

## 🔍 Troubleshooting

**"The tool doesn't open when I double-click it."**  
Make sure you extracted the ZIP file first. Double-clicking the ZIP only shows contents — right-click it and choose "Extract All."

**"The output file is blank."**  
Your image may be too large. Try resizing it to under 2000 pixels wide, then try again.

**"It says 'file not found.'"**  
Make sure you haven't moved or deleted the app folder while it's running. Keep everything together in one place.

**"The art looks blocky or uneven."**  
That's normal for complex images. Try lowering the detail level in the settings, or use images with fewer, larger shapes.
